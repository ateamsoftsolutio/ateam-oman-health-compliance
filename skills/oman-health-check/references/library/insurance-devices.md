## How to read this file

- **ID**: a stable identifier for one requirement (for example `PDPL-07`). IDs are not legal references; the legal reference is in the *Source* field.
- **Requirement**: what the rule requires, in plain language.
- **Engineering implication**: what the build team has to design, configure or produce.
- **Source**: the instrument, plus the article or clause number **only where it was checked** against the text, with a URL.
- **Confidence**:
  - **Confirmed (primary)**: checked against the official text, or its reproduction on qanoon.om with the Official Gazette reference, or a government website or form.
  - **Confirmed (secondary)**: taken from a reputable law-firm guide, academic paper or reputable news, because the primary text could not be read in full.
  - **Unconfirmed**: not verified. Do not build hard logic on it; ask.
- **As of 2026-10**: the status at the research date, including pending changes.
- **Applicability tags** in brackets: **[All]** any system processing health data of people in Oman; **[Gov]** Ministry of Health or other government health facilities and their vendors; **[Private]** private hospitals, clinics and other private providers; **[Insurer]** insurers and TPAs; **[Claims]** provider systems handling eligibility, prior approval or claims; **[Telehealth]**; **[AI]**; **[Device]** software that may be a medical device; **[Research]**; **[Prescribing]**; **[Pharmacy]**; **[HIE]** systems exchanging data with national platforms; **[CNI]** critical national infrastructure; **[Cloud]**.

## Requirements

### A. Health insurance and the Dhamani platform

#### INS-01: Health insurance regulator and legal basis [All]
- **Requirement**: Oman has no standalone health insurance law. Private health insurance is regulated under the Insurance Companies Law (RD 12/79) through CMA decisions. RD 20/2024 created the Financial Services Authority (FSA). All CMA rights and obligations passed to the FSA (Art. 3). Existing laws, regulations and decisions continue to apply until the FSA issues replacements, so far as they are consistent with the new system (Art. 4). The CMA health insurance decisions listed above therefore remain in force under the FSA.
- **Engineering implication**: Name the FSA as the regulator in user-facing text, consent notices and audit reports. Keep the original CMA decision numbers (for example E/83/2023) as the legal references in the requirements library. Add a monitoring task to check FSA decisions each month, because any of these CMA instruments can be replaced.
- **Source**: RD 20/2024, Arts. 1, 3 and 4 (Arabic; our translation), https://qanoon.om/p/2024/rd2024020/. FSA Decision E/12/2026 recitals (still cite RD 12/79, 16 Aug 2026), https://qanoon.om/p/2026/fsa20269906/.
- **Confidence**: Confirmed (primary)
- **As of 2026-10**: In force. No new insurance or health insurance law found.

#### INS-02: Who must be insured, and the status of the mandate [All]
- **Requirement**: The Unified Policy covers "workers" of the policyholder and their dependants: a spouse legally resident in Oman, children up to 21, and other registered dependants resident in Oman (Decision 34/2019, Ch. 1 items 3, 14, 15). Decision 98/2019 added domestic workers, with a two-year policy and its own benefit table. The Visitors' Policy (Decision 144/2022) applies to everyone arriving by land, sea or air to visit. Labour Law Art. 57: if a worker is treated in a government or private hospital and has no health insurance cover, the employer pays for treatment, medicine and the hospital stay. The Council of Ministers approved phased mandatory cover for private-sector workers, expatriates and visitors in 2018 (Atheer, 15 Jul 2018). We found no binding instrument that fixes when each group of employers must insure staff. Lockton (4 Sep 2024) said the mandate was expected once the platform was fully operational by end-2025. Questions for advisers: Is a mandate now legally in force? For which employers (size or sector), from what dates? Does it cover Omanis in the private sector and dependants? Is visitor cover checked at entry?
- **Engineering implication**: Do not hard-code assumptions such as "every expatriate is insured". Treat insurance status as data: check eligibility in Dhamani for every insured encounter and support self-pay. Model member categories (employee, dependant, domestic worker, visitor) and plan tables as configuration, so a later mandate or phase can be switched on without code changes.
- **Source**: Decision 34/2019, Ch. 1 items 3, 14, 15 and Ch. 2 item 5, https://qanoon.om/p/2019/cma20190034/. Decision 98/2019, Arts. 1 to 3, https://qanoon.om/p/2019/cma20190098/. Decision 144/2022, Ch. 2 item 4, https://qanoon.om/p/2022/cma20220144/. RD 53/2023, Art. 57, https://qanoon.om/p/2023/rd2023053/. Atheer, https://www.atheer.om/archive/475293/. Lockton, https://global.lockton.com/us/en/news-insights/oman-updates-compulsory-health-insurance-policy.
- **Confidence**: Unconfirmed (the scope of the mandate and its dates). The policy definitions and Labour Law Art. 57 are confirmed from the primary text.
- **As of 2026-10**: About 650,000 people insured and about 32,000 policies (Times of Oman, 6 May 2026). No mandate start date found.

#### INS-03: Route all health insurance transactions through Dhamani and register on it [Claims] [Insurer]
- **Requirement**: Dhamani is the FSA's electronic platform for health insurance services (E/83/2023, Art. 1). "Health insurance practitioners" are insurers, health service providers, TPAs, RCM companies and any other entity providing health insurance services. They must carry out all health insurance transactions and exchange information through Dhamani, from the date a policy is concluded until its cover ends (Art. 2). They must register on the platform by application to the FSA with company documents, licences and fees (Art. 3). Providers must also be entered on the FSA's register before offering insured services (Decision 78/2019, Art. 4; fee OMR 200, Art. 31) and must apply the electronic link system (Art. 12). FSA platform fees (E/83/2023, Art. 9) include 0.9% of the value of each claim exchanged by providers and RCM companies, and 1% of direct premiums a year for insurers. Penalties: warning with 30 days to correct, fine of OMR 5,000 to 20,000, suspension of access for 30 to 90 days, or deregistration (Art. 10). The FSA told clinics, polyclinics and medical centres to register by 31 March 2025, with all transactions through Dhamani from 1 June 2025; facilities not connected are removed from insurer networks (news reports).
- **Engineering implication**: Make Dhamani the system of record for eligibility, prior approval, claims and remittance for insured patients. Do not build direct payer integrations for these flows. Make Dhamani connectivity a go-live gate for any Omani facility. Store each facility's FSA provider registration and Dhamani credentials, with renewal reminders. Add a fee ledger that calculates 0.9% of each submitted claim and reconciles it against FSA charges. Report any insured transaction not sent through Dhamani as a compliance exception.
- **Source**: E/83/2023, Arts. 1, 2, 3, 9 and 10 (Arabic; our translation), https://qanoon.om/p/2023/cma20230083k/. Decision 78/2019, Arts. 4, 12 and 31, https://qanoon.om/p/2019/cma20190078/. Deadlines: Oman Observer, 26 Mar 2025, https://www.omanobserver.om/article/1168248/business/markets/dhamani-will-enhance-quality-of-services; The Arabian Stories, 11 Mar 2025, https://www.thearabianstories.com/2025/03/11/private-health-facilities-in-oman-must-join-dhamani-platform-by-end-of-march/.
- **Confidence**: Confirmed (primary). The March and June 2025 deadlines are Confirmed (secondary).
- **As of 2026-10**: In force. Sources differ on when live use began (late 2024 or January 2025); the FSA announced the official launch on 20 April 2025. FSA reports more than 20 million transactions since launch (Muscat Daily, 7 Oct 2026).

#### INS-04: Use the medical coding the FSA specifies [Claims] [Insurer]
- **Requirement**: Every Dhamani user must use the medical coding the FSA specifies for all platform transactions (E/83/2023, Art. 4(2)). RCM companies must use the FSA-approved medical coding licence in their systems (E/107/2022, Art. 17). They must employ at least three medical coders holding the FSA-specified coding licence (Art. 6(6)), check that diagnosis and treatment data match the approved codes (Art. 21(3)), and must not work with a coding licence the FSA has not approved (Art. 22(6)). In 2022 the CMA adopted the American Medical Association's CPT code set for Dhamani and licensed it for platform users. We could not confirm the diagnosis code set and version, or the drug code set.
- **Engineering implication**: Build code-set management: load CPT and the FSA-specified diagnosis and drug code sets, version them, and validate every prior-approval request and claim before submission. Keep a mapping from local charge codes to FSA codes. Check whether your product needs its own CPT distribution licence from the AMA; the FSA licence covers platform use, and we did not confirm that it covers third-party software. Get the code-set specification from the FSA Dhamani team before design freeze.
- **Source**: E/83/2023, Art. 4(2), https://qanoon.om/p/2023/cma20230083k/. E/107/2022, Arts. 1(9), 6(6), 17, 21(3) and 22(6), https://qanoon.om/p/2022/cma20220107k/. CPT: Oman Observer, 2 Feb 2022, https://www.omanobserver.om/article/1113622/business/dhamani-health-insurance-platform-to-adopt-ama-system; Shabiba, 12 Jan 2022, https://shabiba.com/article/169691-سوق-المال-تطلق-أولى-البرامج-التدريبية-في-مجال-الترميز-الطبي.
- **Confidence**: Confirmed (primary). CPT adoption is Confirmed (secondary).
- **As of 2026-10**: In force. Diagnosis and drug code sets unconfirmed (see INS-16).

#### INS-05: Data, security and downtime duties of Dhamani users [Claims] [Insurer]
- **Requirement**: Each Dhamani user must (E/83/2023, Art. 4): tell the insured person why their personal data is collected, and of their right to access it and ask for updates through the platform (item 1); take full responsibility for actions done through the platform (item 3); keep information exchanged through the platform confidential and disclose it only with FSA permission (item 4); notify the FSA immediately of any security incident or data leak on the platform (item 5); have staff who process the data sign a confidentiality undertaking (item 6); provide qualified people and technical resources (item 7); and settle amounts due between users through the platform (item 8). If the platform is partly down, users must handle emergency treatment directly (Art. 5). Users must not access the platform or any connected server, database or computer without authorisation, and must not upload files with viruses or corrupted data (Art. 6). The FSA may inspect use of the platform and demand any documents or data (Art. 8).
- **Engineering implication**: Show a privacy notice covering Dhamani data use, access and update rights at registration (align it with the PDPL notice covered elsewhere). Give each user a named account and log every Dhamani action to that person. Add an "inform the FSA immediately" step to the incident runbook for anything touching Dhamani data. Record staff confidentiality undertakings in onboarding. Scan and validate attachments before upload. Build a downtime mode: emergency care proceeds, transactions queue and are submitted when Dhamani returns, and emergency cases are flagged.
- **Source**: E/83/2023, Arts. 4, 5, 6 and 8 (Arabic; our translation), https://qanoon.om/p/2023/cma20230083k/.
- **Confidence**: Confirmed (primary)
- **As of 2026-10**: In force. The regulation sets no deadline for the FSA notification beyond "immediately".

#### INS-06: Prior authorisation rules and 30-minute response times [Claims] [Insurer]
- **Requirement**: Providers must get prior approval from the insurer before treatment, in line with Appendix 5 of the Unified Policy (Ch. 3 item 3). Approval is needed (Appendix 5, item 1) for outpatient care when the total cost of consultations, laboratory, diagnosis, radiology, medicines or procedures exceeds OMR 100, and for non-emergency inpatient care and surgery. In an emergency, treatment starts at once and the request is sent within 48 hours of admission (item 2). The request must include all necessary medical information and the date and time of the request (item 3). The insurer must confirm receipt immediately (item 7) and reply within 30 minutes. A refusal must give reasons. No reply within 30 minutes counts as implied approval, once receipt within that time is confirmed (items 4 and 5). The provider must answer insurer queries within 30 minutes (item 6). TPAs must complete prior approvals within the policy's time limits (E/34/2020, Art. 20(3)).
- **Engineering implication**: Provider systems: a rules engine that flags the OMR 100 outpatient threshold and non-emergency admissions; an emergency flag with a 48-hour timer; timestamps on every request; tracking of the receipt acknowledgement; 30-minute timers that record implied approval when they expire; a 30-minute timer and alert for insurer queries. Payer and TPA systems: automatic acknowledgement, reason codes required for refusals, and an SLA monitor that escalates before 30 minutes.
- **Source**: Decision 34/2019, Ch. 3 items 1(a) and 3, Appendix 5 items 1 to 7 (Arabic; our translation), https://qanoon.om/p/2019/cma20190034/. E/34/2020, Art. 20(3), https://qanoon.om/p/2020/cma20200034k/.
- **Confidence**: Confirmed (primary)
- **As of 2026-10**: In force. The text does not say whether the OMR 100 total is per visit or per episode. We do not know whether Dhamani enforces implied approval itself (see INS-16).

#### INS-07: Claims content, rejections and payment deadlines [Claims] [Insurer]
- **Requirement**: A provider's claim notification follows the insurer's form and attaches full medical and diagnostic reports, the prior-authorisation form and any other necessary medical information (Decision 34/2019, Ch. 3 item 1(a)). All claim notifications must be compatible with the electronic claims systems in force in Oman (item 1(b)). Consultant fees are payable only with a referral from a licensed doctor (item 2; Decision 78/2019, Art. 22(2)). The insurer may reject or return claims for the listed reasons, including an incomplete form, no doctor's signature and stamp, tests not ordered by a doctor, diagnosis unrelated to treatment, submission more than 120 days after treatment, limits exceeded, or dates outside cover (item 5). When rejecting a claim from an insured person, the insurer must give written reasons within 10 days of the claim's submission. Insurers pay providers within 45 days of receiving the claim unless otherwise agreed (78/2019, Art. 19(7)). Reimbursable expenses are paid to the insured within 15 working days of complete documents, which must arrive within 120 working days (Ch. 3 item 4). Rejected, returned or reduced claims can go to the FSA's health insurance disputes committee (78/2019, Art. 24(2)). Claims cannot be heard more than one year after the policy ends (Art. 11). Providers must not manipulate medical records or financial statements (Art. 22(1)).
- **Engineering implication**: In the claims engine, enforce the 120-day submission window with early warnings. Require a referral reference for consultant visits and a prior-approval reference where needed. Provide an electronic doctor sign-off that Dhamani accepts as "signature and stamp" (ask the FSA what it accepts). Use structured rejection reason codes mapped to the policy grounds. Track the 45-day and 15-working-day payment deadlines. Export a dispute pack (claim, approvals, correspondence). Keep claims, remittance and audit logs at least until one year after policy end, and insurers' financial records for 10 years (see INS-11). Make clinical records append-only with audit trails.
- **Source**: Decision 34/2019, Ch. 3 items 1, 2, 4 and 5, https://qanoon.om/p/2019/cma20190034/. Decision 78/2019, Arts. 11, 19(7), 22(1), 22(2) and 24, https://qanoon.om/p/2019/cma20190078/. E/83/2023, Art. 4(8), https://qanoon.om/p/2023/cma20230083k/.
- **Confidence**: Confirmed (primary)
- **As of 2026-10**: In force.

#### INS-08: Identity and eligibility checks before treatment [Claims]
- **Requirement**: The provider must verify the insured person's identity before treatment. It loses the right to claim if a service was given to someone who is not the insured (Decision 78/2019, Art. 22(3)). RCM companies must verify identity and entitlement for the providers they serve (E/107/2022, Art. 21(2)). On request, TPAs must give providers a detailed electronic list of insured persons with names, residence, ages, gender, nationality, phone numbers and cover start and end dates, and must tell providers the co-payment or deductible amounts (E/34/2020, Art. 21). Insured persons must report lost or damaged insurance cards (78/2019, Art. 21(2)). Dhamani handled 2.3 million eligibility checks in the first half of 2025.
- **Engineering implication**: At registration, capture the civil or resident ID and run a real-time Dhamani eligibility check, and repeat it before each billable service. Store the eligibility response, with timestamp and co-payment data, against the encounter as evidence. Block insured billing when verification fails. Show co-payment and deductible to the cashier. Handle lost-card flags.
- **Source**: Decision 78/2019, Arts. 21(2) and 22(3), https://qanoon.om/p/2019/cma20190078/. E/107/2022, Art. 21(2), https://qanoon.om/p/2022/cma20220107k/. E/34/2020, Art. 21, https://qanoon.om/p/2020/cma20200034k/. Statistics: Times of Oman, 27 Jun 2025, https://timesofoman.com/article/159776-omans-dhamani-platform-revolutionises-health-insurance-with-record-activity-in-2025.
- **Confidence**: Confirmed (primary)
- **As of 2026-10**: In force.

#### INS-09: Benefits, cost sharing and membership rules of the Unified Policy [Insurer] [Claims]
- **Requirement**: Every policy must give at least the basic benefits in Appendix 4 (Decision 78/2019, Art. 2). Table A: inpatient limit OMR 3,000 per insured per policy period, 30 days per admission, shared room, ambulance up to OMR 100 per transfer; outpatient limit OMR 500 per period, with co-payment of 15% in the specified network (capped at OMR 20 per visit) or 30% outside it; pharmacy co-payment 10% (capped at OMR 5 per visit), generic medicines first; repatriation of remains up to OMR 1,000. Table B has the same limits and no co-payment column. Table C, for domestic workers (Decision 98/2019): inpatient limit OMR 4,000, outpatient OMR 500, repatriation OMR 1,000, two-year policy. The sub-limits add up to OMR 4,500, but the text sets no separate overall limit. Optional benefits (Appendix 3) include maternity, dental, optical, child health and work injuries. Chapter 4 lists exclusions. The schedule adds a supervision fee of 6 per thousand and an emergency fund fee of 1% of net premium (Appendix 1). The policyholder keeps a register of insured staff with name, gender, age, nationality, marital status and card number (Ch. 2 item 4). Additions and deletions need written notice; refunds follow a pro-rata formula and are paid within 30 days (Ch. 2 item 8).
- **Engineering implication**: Build a benefits engine with plan tables A, B, C and visitor, per-admission day caps, per-visit co-payment caps, network tiers, a generic-first pharmacy rule and the exclusion list. Keep accumulators per insured per policy period. Calculate premium with the supervision and emergency fund fees. Build member add and delete with the pro-rata refund formula and a 30-day refund timer, and an employer roster with the required fields.
- **Source**: Decision 34/2019, Ch. 2 items 2, 4, 6, 8 and 10, Ch. 4, Appendices 1, 3 and 4 (Arabic; our translation), https://qanoon.om/p/2019/cma20190034/. Decision 98/2019, Arts. 1 to 3, https://qanoon.om/p/2019/cma20190098/. Decision 78/2019, Art. 2, https://qanoon.om/p/2019/cma20190078/.

#### INS-10: TPA system duties [Insurer]
- **Requirement**: Insurers and providers may deal only with licensed TPAs (E/34/2020, Art. 2). Insurers must contract a TPA unless they run their own claims system (Decision 78/2019, Art. 19(8)). A TPA licence application includes a detailed report on the electronic system and how it is protected (E/34/2020, Art. 8(8)). A TPA must: link its system to the FSA's electronic health insurance system and any other system the FSA names (Art. 16); protect its system and data from intrusion and unauthorised access, have data recovery in place, and keep the system running through emergencies and faults (Art. 17); keep all data strictly confidential, disclosing only with the FSA's explicit permission, the data subject's explicit consent, a legal requirement or a court order (Art. 19); audit claims and check cover before passing claims to the insurer, and finish prior approvals within policy time limits (Art. 20); give providers the member list and co-payment information in INS-08 (Art. 21). A TPA must not pay providers on the insurer's behalf (Art. 22(5)). It reports fraud and misuse of benefits to the FSA every quarter (Art. 27). Fines: OMR 1,000 for breaching Arts. 16 to 18 and OMR 3,000 for Arts. 5, 19 and 22 (Arts. 31 and 33).
- **Engineering implication**: For TPA platforms: Dhamani integration; a written security architecture that can be filed with the licence; tested backup, restore and disaster recovery; high availability; a disclosure log recording the legal basis of every third-party disclosure; claim-scrubbing and cover-check rules; a member eligibility feed or API for providers with the listed fields; no payment disbursement functions; a quarterly fraud and misuse report.
- **Source**: E/34/2020, Arts. 2, 8(8), 16, 17, 19 to 22, 27, 31 and 33 (Arabic; our translation), https://qanoon.om/p/2020/cma20200034k/. Decision 78/2019, Art. 19(8), https://qanoon.om/p/2019/cma20190078/.
- **Confidence**: Confirmed (primary)
- **As of 2026-10**: In force. A 2021 law-firm note (BSA) said Oman does not license TPAs; E/34/2020 shows that is out of date.

#### INS-11: RCM companies must operate inside Oman [Claims]
- **Requirement**: Revenue cycle management means organising documents and submitting, following up and processing insurance claims for a provider (E/107/2022, Art. 1(3)). Only licensed RCM companies may do it, and providers, insurers and TPAs must not deal with unlicensed ones (Art. 2). An RCM company must: link to the FSA system (Art. 16); use the FSA-approved coding licence (Art. 17); protect its system, recover lost data and stay running (Art. 18); carry out all operations related to its activity inside Oman and keep all data confidential (Art. 20). It is prohibited from carrying out RCM operations outside Oman (Art. 22(3)). Its duties to the provider include running the provider's electronic system, checking identity and entitlement, checking codes, fixing rejected claims, training staff, sending claims electronically, following up payments, documenting all services and collecting co-payments (Art. 21). It reports fraud and misuse every four months (Art. 27). Fines: OMR 3,000 (Arts. 16 to 19) and OMR 7,000 (Arts. 5, 6, 20 and 22).
- **Engineering implication**: Offshore RCM processing (coding, claim submission, denial work) for Omani providers is not allowed for licensed RCM companies. Host RCM workloads and run RCM operations in Oman. Restrict offshore engineering access to production RCM data, and ask an adviser whether remote vendor support counts as "operations outside Oman". RCM products need coder-licence checks, code validation, denial workflows, co-payment collection and a four-monthly fraud report.
- **Source**: E/107/2022, Arts. 1(3), 2, 16 to 18, 20 to 22, 27 and 31 to 33 (Arabic; our translation), https://qanoon.om/p/2022/cma20220107k/.
- **Confidence**: Confirmed (primary)
- **As of 2026-10**: In force.

#### INS-12: Insurer licensing conditions on IT, data security and records [Insurer]
- **Requirement**: An application for a health insurance licence must include an information technology policy and a data security and confidentiality policy, a claims management policy (Decision 78/2019, Art. 13(1)(f) and (g)), and an undertaking to have an electronic system (Art. 13(2)(c)). Licences last two years (Art. 18). The insurer must keep the insured person's data confidential and protect it (Art. 19(2)). It must give the FSA any information it asks for, including databases and records (Art. 19(5)(b)), and keep health insurance financial records for 10 years (Art. 19(6)). The insurer must keep claim supporting documents confidential (Decision 34/2019, Ch. 2 item 11). A licence for co-insurance needs an information security policy (Decision 58/2024, Art. 5(4)).
- **Engineering implication**: Write and maintain IT and data security policies that map to these licence conditions. Retain health insurance financial records for 10 years, with legal hold. Build a regulator export of databases and records. Restrict access to claim supporting documents to named roles.
- **Source**: Decision 78/2019, Arts. 13, 18 and 19 (Arabic; our translation), https://qanoon.om/p/2019/cma20190078/. Decision 34/2019, Ch. 2 item 11, https://qanoon.om/p/2019/cma20190034/. Decision 58/2024, Art. 5(4), https://qanoon.om/p/2024/fsa20240058/.

#### INS-13: Rules for insurer and broker digital channels [Insurer]
- **Requirement**: Insurers and brokers need FSA approval before offering insurance electronically (E/80/2023, Art. 3). Insurers must build a platform for marketing and selling policies, collecting premiums, receiving claims and handling complaints (Art. 4). The approval file includes a risk analysis, a contingency plan with backups, information security controls, payment protection technology and any third-party contract (Art. 5). Before selling, they must verify customer identity, keep identity records for at least 10 years, secure account access and verify phone and email with an activation link (Art. 9). Customers get an electronic record with the full policy, premium and claim status (Art. 10(3)). Electronic forms must match the FSA-approved version (Art. 10(6)). Renewal notices go out at least one month before expiry (Art. 11(3)). Claims are lodged on the platform and get a reference number (Art. 12). The platform must be independently audited at least every two years, or yearly if the FSA requires, with the report filed (Art. 16). It must be available around the clock; maintenance downtime may not exceed 24 hours, and longer outages must be reported to the FSA immediately (Art. 20). Platform management may be outsourced only to a third party with premises in Oman, licensed by the relevant authorities, with qualified staff, where the insurer or broker owns the platform and the FSA approves (Art. 21). Other duties: a board-approved security policy filed with the FSA (Art. 23); no disclosure without FSA written approval (Art. 24); protection of personal data from loss, unauthorised access, change or disclosure (Art. 25); Central Bank of Oman-approved payment systems (Art. 26); a security and privacy notice on the platform (Art. 27). Penalties include a fine of OMR 2,000 and suspension of the platform for up to 30 days (Art. 28).
- **Engineering implication**: Use this as the feature and operations checklist for insurer portals and apps. Set a 24/7 service objective with maintenance windows under 24 hours. Plan a third-party platform audit every two years. A vendor without Omani premises and a licence cannot manage the platform, so plan an Omani entity or partner. Make sure the insurer owns the platform in contract (consider code escrow). Use CBO-approved payment gateways. Build phone and email activation links and 10-year retention of identity records.
- **Source**: E/80/2023, Decision Art. 2 and Regulation Arts. 3 to 5, 9 to 13, 15, 16, 20, 21 and 23 to 28 (Arabic; our translation), https://qanoon.om/p/2023/cma20230080k/.

#### INS-14: Visitor cover, the visitors' emergency levy and co-insurance [Insurer]
- **Requirement**: Visitors' Policy (Decision 144/2022): cover starts on entry to Oman; the premium is paid in full before entry (Ch. 2 items 3 and 5). The visitor may cancel within 3 working days if they do not enter, with the premium returned within 5 working days (item 8). Policy administration: calculate the 0.5% levy per policy and produce the quarterly electronic statement in the FSA format (ask the FSA for it). For co-insurance, model participants, shares, premium splits, joint-account reconciliation and loss reports, and limit shared data to the fields the contract lists.

### B. Medical devices, including software and AI

#### MDV-01: Medical devices must be registered with the MOH before supply [Device]
- **Requirement**: The Pharmacy Law regulates "medical supplies", the legal term covering medical devices (RD 35/2015, Art. 1). A technical committee decides on registering device companies and their products (Art. 27(e)), and the Executive Regulation sets the conditions (Art. 32). No medicine or medical supply, made locally or imported, may be circulated before it is registered with the Ministry (MD 113/2020, Art. 3). Device companies must be registered (Art. 80) and each device registered (Arts. 81 and 82). A registration certificate lasts five years (Art. 84) and must be renewed at least six months before expiry (Art. 85). Imports must be registered and match the registered specification (Art. 86). The law names the "Directorate General of Pharmacy and Drug Control" as the competent body (RD 35/2015, Art. 1). In practice the regulator is now the MOH Drug Safety Center (DSC), whose Medical Device Department handles devices (MD 135/2025, Art. 1; DSC guidelines and circulars). Enforcement has been phased in; see MDV-04 and MDV-05.
- **Engineering implication**: If your software is a medical device (MDV-02), treat Oman registration as a release gate and appoint an Omani local agent. Keep a regulatory register per product with the Oman certificate number, expiry date and a renewal reminder six months ahead.
- **Source**: RD 35/2015, Arts. 1, 27 and 32, https://qanoon.om/p/2015/rd2015035/. MD 113/2020, Arts. 3, 80 to 86, https://qanoon.om/p/2020/moh20200113/ (Arabic; our translation). MD 135/2025, Art. 1, https://qanoon.om/p/2025/moh20250135/. GD3 (Oct 2025), introduction, which says regulation is enforced through MD 113/2020.
- **Confidence**: Confirmed (primary)
- **As of 2026-10**: In force. We did not find the instrument that created the DSC from the former Directorate General of Pharmaceutical Affairs and Drug Control.

#### MDV-02: Deciding whether your software is a medical device [Device] [AI]
- **Requirement**: The law defines medical supplies as any device, means, material, instrument or product, used alone or with others, "including the software necessary for its use", for humans, for treatment, diagnosis, prevention or alleviation of disease, analysis, compensation for injury, regulating pregnancy and similar purposes (RD 35/2015, Art. 1). The DSC's guidelines use the wider GHTF/IMDRF definition, which lists "software" as a device in its own right when the manufacturer intends it for diagnosis, prevention, monitoring, treatment or alleviation of disease or injury, and similar purposes (GD3 2025, GD-22 and the 2026 reporting guideline). The e-IFU guideline (GD-23) names "stand-alone software" as in scope. We found no Oman guidance on software qualification, clinical decision support or AI. The draft innovative-device guidance proposes a DSC consultation that starts by deciding whether the product is a medical device; products judged not to be devices fall outside DSC control. CMS (Sept 2025) reports that the MOH first assesses whether an app is a medical device.
- **Engineering implication**: For each product and module, write an intended-use statement and a qualification memo against the GHTF/IMDRF definition. Keep functions that may be device functions (diagnosis or triage suggestions, risk scores, dose calculation, image analysis) in separately versioned modules, so non-device functions (scheduling, billing, claims, records storage) stay out of scope. For borderline cases, ask the DSC (med-device@moh.gov.om) and file its answer.
- **Source**: RD 35/2015, Art. 1, https://qanoon.om/p/2015/rd2015035/. GD3 (Oct 2025), Definitions, https://www.moh.gov.om/media/ov3mnntj/guidance-document-gd3-guideline-on-requirements-of-class-c-d-medical-devices-registration.pdf. GD-23, Scope, https://www.moh.gov.om/media/3jvo5xsp/guidance-document-gd-23-guidline-for-electronic-instraction-for-use.pdf. Draft innovative-device guidance, flowchart (Arabic; our translation), URL in the instrument table. CMS Expert Guide, https://cms.law/en/int/expert-guides/cms-expert-guide-to-digital-health-apps-and-telemedicine/oman.
- **Confidence**: Confirmed (primary) for the definitions. The qualification method is our recommendation.
- **As of 2026-10**: No software-specific guidance. Whether stand-alone software meets the narrower statutory wording is a question for an adviser.

#### MDV-03: Risk class follows your reference jurisdiction [Device] [AI]
- **Requirement**: Oman uses four risk classes, A to D. GD3 covers Class C (moderate-high, stated as "Class IIb / Class III") and Class D (high, stated as "all other Class III / Class IV / AIMD"). The applicant states which regulatory system the device follows: Saudi Arabia (MDMA), USA, EU, Canada, Australia, Japan or other. A wrong class may force re-submission. Fees depend on class. GD3 cites the GHTF classification principles (GHTF/SG1/N77:2012). Secondary sources map Class A to Class I and Class B to Class II/IIa. We found no Oman-specific classification rule for software.
- **Engineering implication**: Classify the software first in your reference jurisdiction (for example under EU MDR or with the FDA), then map it to the Oman class and record the reasoning in the technical file. Many decision-support products will be Class B or C, which decides the Oman route (MDV-04 or MDV-05) and fees.
- **Source**: GD3 (Oct 2025), Scope, General Requirements items 4 and 5, Section 1, https://www.moh.gov.om/media/ov3mnntj/guidance-document-gd3-guideline-on-requirements-of-class-c-d-medical-devices-registration.pdf. RegDesk (modified July 2026) for Classes A and B, https://www.regdesk.co/regulations-library/oman/.
- **Confidence**: Confirmed (primary). The Class A and B mapping is Confirmed (secondary).
- **As of 2026-10**: Current.

#### MDV-04: Register high-risk (Class C and D) devices and their manufacturer by 31 December 2026 [Device]
- **Requirement**: Circular 161/2025 (1 Jul 2025) began mandatory registration of high-risk device manufacturers and products under MD 113/2020 and said shipments would not be released from 1 Jul 2026. Circular 64/2026 (24 Jun 2026) extended the period to 31 Dec 2026. Until then, shipments clear if the importer shows that registration has started or been submitted. After that date, unregistered high-risk devices will not be cleared. Before registering a device, the applicant needs initial approval for wholesale activity, local establishment approval and manufacturer registration (GD3, General Requirements item 3). Manufacturer registration (GD-22) needs a quality management certificate (for example ISO 13485), a recent audit report, the production process, the site layout and a commercial agency certificate. Foreign manufacturers need a local commercial agency. Fees are OMR 500 for a foreign manufacturer (five years) or OMR 1,500 for a local one (three years), with a 60-working-day service time. Device registration takes 60 working days and costs OMR 100 (Class A to B) or OMR 200 (Class C to D). The dossier (GD3) covers device information, including any IT infrastructure, laptop or phone needed to run it; labels and IFU; an essential-principles checklist; design verification and validation; reference-jurisdiction certificates (CE, 510(k), PMA); clinical evidence including software validation; post-market control; distribution status; declaration of conformity; and benefit-risk analysis. Class C omits Sections 5 and 6. Applications are made through the MOH e-Health portal by the legal representative or local agent (MD 113/2020, Art. 82).
- **Engineering implication**: For Class C or D software, build the technical file: software description and architecture, lifecycle and verification and validation records, risk management file, clinical evaluation, essential-principles checklist and reference approvals. Sign up an Omani local agent and submit before 31 Dec 2026 for anything supplied after that date. The circulars speak of "shipment clearance". Ask the DSC how this applies to software delivered by download or from the cloud.
- **Source**: Circular 161/2025, https://moh.gov.om/media/hkadf425/cirno161-of-2025-regarding-commencement-of-registration-of-high-risk-medical-devices-supplies-manufactruers-their-products.pdf (scanned; read from image). Circular 64/2026, https://moh.gov.om/media/zisn2dzq/cirno64-of-2026-regarding-extension-of-registration-period-for-high-risk-medical-devices-and-supplies-manufacturers-and-their-products.pdf (scanned; read from image). GD3 (Oct 2025), Chapters 2 and 3. GD-22 (Oct 2025), Chapter 2. MOH service pages: https://moh.gov.om/en/services/businesses/drug-safety-center/request-for-registration-of-medical-device/ and the manufacturer registration page (Arabic; URL in Sources). MD 113/2020, Art. 82, https://qanoon.om/p/2020/moh20200113/.
- **Confidence**: Confirmed (primary)
- **As of 2026-10**: Extension runs to 31 Dec 2026. Voluntary registration is open.

#### MDV-05: Route for lower-risk (Class A and B) software [Device]
- **Requirement**: Questions for the DSC: MD 113/2020 Art. 3 requires registration of all medical supplies before circulation, but the circulars make registration mandatory only for high-risk devices. The 2021 GD3 said no device may be imported, placed on the market or put into service unless it is listed in the Medical Device Control Department database, and GD1 (2019) sets the listing process: a listing party (manufacturer, authorised representative, importer or distributor), updates within 30 calendar days of a change, and confirmation of accuracy no more than once a year. So: Must Class A and B software be listed, registered, or neither, before supply? Through which portal (MOH e-Health portal or the GHC's Ta'awon 2.0)? When will registration become mandatory for Classes A and B?
- **Engineering implication**: Plan on at least listing through an Omani local agent, and budget for full registration. Keep a GMDN code and device identifiers ready, and a process to update the listing within 30 days of a change.

#### MDV-06: Changes to a registered device need prior approval [Device] [AI]
- **Requirement**: No change or modification may be made to a registered medical supply without the DSC's prior approval (MD 113/2020, Art. 92). After registration, changes go through the DSC variation service (GD3 2025 and GD-22 notes). Registration is cancelled if the device's use is stopped on the advice of the reference regulators, if it has harmful side effects, or if its registration is cancelled or production stopped in the country of origin (Art. 93). We found no Oman guidance on which software changes count, or on pre-agreed change plans for AI models.
- **Engineering implication**: Add an Oman step to release management: classify each change (bug fix, feature, algorithm or model retraining, user interface, security patch) for its Oman variation impact before it reaches Omani users. Be able to hold a release for Oman only. Treat AI model updates as changes needing assessment. Monitor your reference-jurisdiction status, because cancellation there triggers cancellation in Oman.
- **Source**: MD 113/2020, Arts. 92 and 93 (Arabic; our translation), https://qanoon.om/p/2020/moh20200113/. GD3 (Oct 2025), General Requirements note. GD-22 (Oct 2025), Chapter 2 note.
- **Confidence**: Confirmed (primary)
- **As of 2026-10**: In force. Which software changes need approval is a question for the DSC.

#### MDV-07: Incident reporting, post-market surveillance and traceability [Device]
- **Requirement**: Manufacturers or their representatives must tell the Ministry of any safety alert or malfunction and take corrective action (MD 113/2020, Art. 89). Users must report incidents within one month (Art. 90). Importers and local agents keep traceability records of who they sold to and act on safety alerts (Art. 88). The DSC can stop use and withdraw unsafe devices (Art. 91). The July 2026 reporting guideline sets deadlines by severity: 2 working days for a serious public health threat, 10 working days for an unexpected death or serious unexpected injury, and 30 working days for other events. Manufacturers report through their local agent. Investigations end with a final report within 15 days (no testing), 30 days (testing in Oman) or 90 days (testing abroad). Manufacturers, importers and distributors must keep a tracking system for supply and distribution. "Use error", including slips and foreseeable misuse, is in scope. Reports go to vigilance-md@moh.gov.om on the DSC form. GD3: manufacturers prepare a periodic safety update report (PSUR) for each device, and for Class C and D update it at least yearly.
- **Engineering implication**: Build complaint intake into the product. Triage with severity categories mapped to the 2, 10 and 30 working-day deadlines. Add a workflow to notify the Omani local agent. Keep templates for initial, follow-up and final reports, with 15, 30 and 90-day timers. Keep deployment records of which software version runs at which customer site. Generate the yearly PSUR for Class C and D. Be able to carry out field safety corrective actions (remote disable or rollback, customer notices).
- **Source**: MD 113/2020, Arts. 88 to 91, https://qanoon.om/p/2020/moh20200113/. Guideline on Medical Device Reporting (July 2026), Chapters 2 and 3, https://www.moh.gov.om/media/nm5jpbh5/medical-device-reporting-guidance-20072026.pdf. GD3 (Oct 2025), Section 9.
- **Confidence**: Confirmed (primary)
- **As of 2026-10**: The July 2026 guideline replaces GD15 (2022). The one-month limit in Art. 90 and the guideline's shorter deadlines both apply; use the shorter ones.

#### MDV-08: Labels, instructions for use, electronic IFU and promotion [Device]
- **Requirement**: Device registration needs the label and the instructions for use in Arabic and English (MD 113/2020, Art. 82(1)(a) and (b)). GD-23 lets manufacturers give electronic instructions for use (e-IFU) instead of paper for professional-use devices, including stand-alone software. Devices for lay users are excluded. Conditions: all earlier e-IFU versions and their publication dates on the website; packaging or the device tells users the IFU is electronic and gives the web address; content identical to paper; Arabic and English; free, with no account or password; protected against intrusion; downtime kept to a minimum; a documented risk assessment covering users, environment, outages, tampering and emergencies; paper on request without undue delay or cost; e-IFU kept for 15 years after the last device is made (devices with no expiry date); version control under the quality system. No advertising or promotional material for a registered device may be published without prior DSC approval (Art. 96).
- **Engineering implication**: Ship Arabic and English IFU inside the software and on a public, unauthenticated web page with a version history. Keep every IFU version for 15 years. Put IFU changes under change control. Write the e-IFU risk assessment. For apps used by patients (lay users), ask the DSC what IFU form is acceptable. Get DSC approval for marketing pages, app-store listings and in-app promotion before publishing.
- **Source**: MD 113/2020, Arts. 82 and 96, https://qanoon.om/p/2020/moh20200113/. GD-23 (released 10 May 2026), Scope and Chapter 2, https://www.moh.gov.om/media/3jvo5xsp/guidance-document-gd-23-guidline-for-electronic-instraction-for-use.pdf.
- **Confidence**: Confirmed (primary)
- **As of 2026-10**: Current. GD-23's scope cites old EU directives, so its exact boundaries are unclear.

#### MDV-09: AI and cybersecurity: no Oman-specific requirements found [Device] [AI]
- **Requirement**: Questions for the DSC: We found no Oman law, regulation or DSC guidance specific to AI-enabled devices, adaptive algorithms or device cybersecurity. The GD3 essential principles say devices with programmable systems, including software, must ensure repeatability, reliability and performance for their intended use, and reduce the risks of a single fault (Annex 2, 5.12.1). GD3 asks for "software validation (if applicable)" (Section 8). The 2021 GD3 also asked for a description of the software's data interpretation method, "namely the algorithm". The draft innovative-device guidance proposes pre-submission advice, priority review and conditional approval in a technical "sandbox"; it is undated and marked as a draft. Will the DSC ask for AI evidence (training data, bias testing, performance monitoring, change control plans)? Will it ask for cybersecurity evidence (threat model, software bill of materials, patching policy)? Is the innovative pathway in force?
- **Engineering implication**: Keep evidence to the standard of your strictest reference regulator, ready for DSC requests: an algorithm description, dataset documentation, performance on Omani or GCC populations where possible, cybersecurity risk management, a software bill of materials and a patch policy.
- **Source**: GD3 (Oct 2025), Section 8 and Annex 2. GD3 (2021), 7.6.1.6, https://moh.gov.om/media/b3zjgbmb/gd3.pdf. Draft Guidance on Innovative Medical Devices (Arabic; our translation), URL in the instrument table.
- **Confidence**: Unconfirmed
- **As of 2026-10**: No AI or device-cybersecurity guidance published. Innovative-device guidance is still a draft.

#### MDV-10: Gulf Health Council central registration and Ta'awon 2.0 [Device]

In December 2024 it contracted a Bahraini firm to build "Ta'awon", an electronic platform for central GCC registration. ### C. Pharmacy and prescribing

#### RX-01: Prescription-only dispensing and the OTC and GSL lists [Pharmacy] [Prescribing]
- **Requirement**: Medicines may be dispensed only on prescription, except those the Minister lists (MD 113/2020, Art. 4). MD 198/2023 lists over-the-counter (OTC) medicines that only pharmacies may supply without prescription (Annex 1, some with age or use limits) and general sale list (GSL) medicines that shops may sell (Annex 2). Pharmacies must keep to the price and dispensing method in each medicine's registration certificate (MD 113/2020, Art. 2). Only pharmacists and assistant pharmacists may sell or dispense medicines to the public, and only pharmacists may prepare prescriptions (RD 35/2015, Art. 29). Private clinics licensed to keep medicines may dispense only on prescription, except listed medicines, and must keep a prescription register (MD 185/2020, Art. 8(6) and (7)).
- **Engineering implication**: Give every product a dispensing category (prescription, OTC or GSL) and any restriction (for example a minimum age) from MD 198/2023. Block sale of prescription items unless they are linked to a valid prescription. Record the dispensing pharmacist on each sale. For clinic dispensaries, keep an electronic prescription register.
- **Source**: MD 113/2020, Arts. 2 and 4, https://qanoon.om/p/2020/moh20200113/. MD 198/2023, Art. 1 and Annexes 1 and 2, https://qanoon.om/p/2023/moh20230198/. RD 35/2015, Art. 29, https://qanoon.om/p/2015/rd2015035/. MD 185/2020, Art. 8, https://qanoon.om/p/2020/moh20200185/ (all Arabic; our translation).
- **Confidence**: Confirmed (primary)
- **As of 2026-10**: In force.

#### RX-02: What every prescription must contain [Prescribing]
- **Requirement**: A prescription must show (MD 113/2020, Art. 5): the name of the health institution; patient name, age, gender, weight and phone number; the scientific (generic) name of the medicine, quantity and directions for use; the date; and the name, stamp and signature of the treating doctor, plus any other data the DSC sets. Pharmacists stamp dispensed prescriptions with a stamp showing their name and licence number (Art. 35(2)). Pharmacists must report complications from medicines or medical supplies (Art. 8(5)).
- **Engineering implication**: Make all Art. 5 fields mandatory in e-prescribing, including weight and phone number. Prescribe by generic name; brand is optional. Capture prescriber identity and licence number. Provide an electronic equivalent of "stamp and signature", and ask an adviser whether it is valid outside Dhamani; E/83/2023 cites the Electronic Transactions Law (then RD 69/2008, replaced by RD 39/2025 on 14 Apr 2025; see NAT-24 and NAT-25). Record the dispensing pharmacist's name and licence number.
- **Source**: MD 113/2020, Arts. 5, 8(5) and 35(2) (Arabic; our translation), https://qanoon.om/p/2020/moh20200113/.
- **Confidence**: Confirmed (primary)
- **As of 2026-10**: In force.

#### RX-03: Controlled drugs stay on official paper forms [Prescribing] [Pharmacy]
- **Requirement**: The MOH controlled drugs guide (2016) says controlled-drug prescriptions are written on official forms: private institutions buy pads from the regulator; narcotics are pink and psychotropics green. They are written in ink, signed, stamped and dated, show patient name, age and address, directions, dose, form and strength, and give the quantity in words and figures. They are valid for 3 days. Inpatient prescriptions carry one controlled drug each. Only doctors licensed to deal with controlled drugs may prescribe them. The pharmacist endorses the date, stamp and quantity, and keeps used prescriptions for 3 years. Missing or stolen prescriptions are reported within 48 hours. Electronic requisition systems must allow only authorised staff and identify the author of every entry. MD 113/2020 requires a numbered, stamped register of controlled-drug prescriptions (Art. 35(4)), a locked cabinet (Art. 28(9)), a signed handover of narcotics when the pharmacist is absent (Art. 24), and bars assistant pharmacists from dispensing narcotics or psychotropics when in charge (Art. 36). The DSC circulates serial numbers of missing controlled-drug prescriptions (Circular 13/2026). Licences to deal in these substances lapse automatically on expiry, and renewal is due one month before (Circular 66/2026).
- **Engineering implication**: Do not issue electronic controlled-drug prescriptions in place of the official forms unless the DSC confirms an electronic route. Let the EHR record the serial number of the paper form. In pharmacy systems: keep an auditable controlled-drug register, capture the prescription serial number, check the 3-day validity, block dispensing of these drugs while an assistant pharmacist is in charge, check serials against DSC missing-prescription alerts, and warn before licences expire. Keep controlled-drug flags updated when schedules are amended by ministerial decision (for example MD 128/2023).
- **Source**: MOH Guide to Management of Narcotics and Psychotropic Substances (Aug 2016), sections 8A.7, 10A to 10F, https://moh.gov.om/media/apbdi5r4/eng-1.pdf. MD 113/2020, Arts. 24, 28(9), 35(4) and 36, https://qanoon.om/p/2020/moh20200113/. Circular 13/2026 and Circular 66/2026 (scanned; read from image), https://moh.gov.om/en/ministry/drug-safety-center-circulars/. MD 128/2023 (title only checked), https://qanoon.om/p/2023/moh20230128/.
- **Confidence**: Confirmed (primary). The guide is version 1 (2016); we do not know whether a newer one exists.
- **As of 2026-10**: Paper serial-numbered forms were still in use in February 2026 (Circular 13/2026).

#### RX-04: Prices, profit margins and wholesale invoices [Pharmacy]
- **Requirement**: Pharmacies must sell at the price in the medicine's registration certificate (MD 113/2020, Art. 2). Circular 99/2026 (12 Oct 2026) reminds drug stores and private pharmacies that MD 74/2017 sets a total margin of 45%: 19% for agents and wholesalers and 26% for pharmacies and retailers. This applies to all medicines, including those imported under special permits. From 1 Jan 2027, drug stores must print their name exactly as on their licence and their DSC licence number on every sales invoice; new licence applicants must do so now (Circular 73/2026). Drug stores keep records of quantities and batch numbers received and supplied (MD 113/2020, Art. 38(3)). Clinics licensed to dispense must show the set price on each pack (MD 185/2020, Art. 8(4)).
- **Engineering implication**: Sync the price master with MOH-approved prices. Validate margins (19% and 26%) and block manual overrides above them. Update wholesaler invoice templates before 1 Jan 2027. Keep batch-level stock movements.
- **Source**: MD 113/2020, Arts. 2 and 38(3), https://qanoon.om/p/2020/moh20200113/. Circular 99/2026, https://moh.gov.om/en/ministry/drug-safety-center-circulars/cirno99-of-2026-regarding-compliance-with-approved-medicine-profit-margins-under-ministerial-decision-no-742017/. Circular 73/2026, https://moh.gov.om/en/ministry/drug-safety-center-circulars/mandatory-inclusion-of-the-drug-store-name-and-license-number-on-sales-invoices/. MD 185/2020, Art. 8(4), https://qanoon.om/p/2020/moh20200185/. MD 74/2017 itself not read.
- **Confidence**: Confirmed (primary)
- **As of 2026-10**: Invoice rule applies from 1 Jan 2027.

#### RX-05: Promoting medicines in apps and websites needs a DSC licence [Pharmacy]
- **Requirement**: An advertisement is any written, audio, visual or other material that advertises or introduces a medicine, directly or indirectly (MD 135/2025, Art. 1). Apart from scientific offices, only drug companies, local agents and pharmaceutical consultancy offices may advertise medicines, and only with a DSC licence (Art. 2). The medicine must be registered; the content must match its leaflet and summary of product characteristics, name the target group and not mislead (Art. 3). A licence lasts three months and can be renewed (Art. 6). Licensed advertisements must show the licence number, use the approved wording, not be changed without approval, and be "limited to pharmacists" (Art. 7(1) to (4); the meaning of item 4 is unclear in Arabic). Prescription medicines may be promoted only in scientific journals or conferences, or in direct meetings with pharmacists and health professionals (Art. 7(5)).
- **Engineering implication**: In pharmacy and e-commerce apps, do not show promotional content for medicines (banners, push messages, featured placements) unless it is a DSC-licensed advertisement from a permitted advertiser, shows the licence number and is within its three-month validity. Never promote prescription medicines to the public. Build a content-approval workflow with licence number and expiry fields. Neutral catalogue listings may also count as "introducing" a medicine; ask an adviser.
- **Source**: MD 135/2025, Arts. 1 to 7 (Arabic; our translation), https://qanoon.om/p/2025/moh20250135/.
- **Confidence**: Confirmed (primary)
- **As of 2026-10**: In force from 2 Jun 2025. The DSC also consulted on a "Guideline on Advertising of Medicines" until 10 Dec 2025; we did not find a final version.

#### RX-06: Online ordering and home delivery of medicines [Pharmacy]
- **Requirement**: Questions for advisers: A pharmacy is a "place prepared and licensed" for preparing or selling medicines (RD 35/2015, Art. 1). MD 113/2020 sets premises rules for pharmacies (Arts. 28 and 29), requires a phone, computer and internet in each pharmacy (Art. 28(10)) and caps a pharmacy at 20 branches (Art. 31). We found no rule that licenses online pharmacies, app-based ordering or home delivery. Can a licensed pharmacy take orders through an app and deliver them? Can a Dhamani e-prescription be dispensed remotely and delivered? Are controlled drugs and cold-chain items excluded? What records apply to deliveries?
- **Engineering implication**: Until answered, build any ordering app as a channel of a named licensed pharmacy: a licensed pharmacist reviews every order, prescriptions are checked, OTC age limits are applied, cold-chain items are logged at 2 to 8°C, and controlled drugs are not delivered.
- **Source**: RD 35/2015, Art. 1, https://qanoon.om/p/2015/rd2015035/. MD 113/2020, Arts. 28, 29 and 31, https://qanoon.om/p/2020/moh20200113/.
- **Confidence**: Unconfirmed
- **As of 2026-10**: No specific rule found.

#### RX-07: Drug codes, formulary and barcodes [Pharmacy] [Prescribing] [Claims]
- **Requirement**: Questions: Pharmacies must keep "the Omani medicines guide approved by the Ministry" (MD 113/2020, Art. 35(3)) and stock the essential medicines on the DSC's list (Art. 33). The DSC posts the Gulf Health Council's Drug Barcoding Specifications, which ask manufacturers to use GS1 DataMatrix barcodes carrying GTIN (AI 01), expiry (AI 17), batch (AI 10) and serial number (AI 21). Dhamani has a "Master Drug Register". Is there an official machine-readable Omani drug list with codes? Which drug codes does Dhamani use for e-prescriptions and pharmacy claims? Is the GS1 barcode specification mandatory in Oman, and is serial-level verification required?
- **Engineering implication**: Design the drug master to hold several identifiers (MOH registration number, Dhamani drug code, GTIN) and parse GS1 DataMatrix (GTIN, expiry, batch, serial) at receipt and dispensing. Confirm the authoritative source before building feeds.
- **Source**: MD 113/2020, Arts. 33 and 35(3), https://qanoon.om/p/2020/moh20200113/. GHC Drug Barcoding Specifications, https://www.moh.gov.om/media/2usbjt4v/ghc-drug-barcoding-specifications.pdf. Oman Observer, 14 Aug 2023 (Master Drug Register), https://www.omanobserver.om/article/1141438/business/economy/dhamani-health-insurance-e-platform-fully-in-place-cma.
- **Confidence**: Unconfirmed
- **As of 2026-10**: Unknown.
